## Proposed Central Claim

Across real MiP-NeRF 360 scenes and controlled synthetic stimuli, independently trained splatting systems show reproducible structure-dependent reconstruction behavior: local performance is not fully explained by a single global metric, and pairwise method gaps can be analyzed as functions of controlled or measured image structure. The completed evidence supports observational local complementarity and controlled performance-gap characterization, not causal attribution to kernel family alone.

## Proposed Contributions

1. A family-agnostic local comparison pipeline for splatting systems, including GT alignment auditing, patchwise errors, tie-aware winner maps, structural descriptors, oracle headroom, sensitivity analysis, and reusable cross-scene aggregation.
2. A three-family empirical characterization of 3DGS, GES, and DRK on Garden, Bicycle, and Room showing replicated local complementarity and descriptor-associated winner regimes.
3. A controlled synthetic benchmark that independently manipulates edge sharpness, spatial frequency, and corrected curvature while fixing cameras, lighting, scale, resolution, and train/test protocol.
4. A five-seed matched statistical analysis framework that treats scene realization, not view or patch, as the independent unit for testing whether method gaps change across controlled factor levels.

## Evidence Chain

Real-scene local specialization establishes the phenomenon: across Garden, Bicycle, and Room, different methods win different patch subsets, and oracle selection has measurable MSE headroom over fixed 3DGS.

Structural dependence gives the phenomenon shape: winner probabilities and descriptor medians vary consistently with GT-derived image structure, especially the lower-complexity DRK regime and several higher-complexity GES trends.

Controlled synthetic manipulation reduces ambiguity: edge sharpness, spatial frequency, and curvature are varied one at a time under fixed cameras, lighting, scene scale, image resolution, and train/test counts. The corrected curvature protocol removes the known foreground-occupancy confound.

Multi-seed statistical replication provides the formal test layer: five matched seeds per condition allow pairwise method gaps to be tested across low/medium/high levels using seed-level repeated measures rather than treating views or patches as independent.

Together, the chain supports a careful paper about structure-dependent reconstruction behavior and measurement methodology. It does not yet justify a causal claim that kernel family alone determines local specialization.

## Important Negative Results

- DRK globally winning all controlled synthetic scenes, if confirmed in `phase3_results.csv/json`, means the synthetic pilot does not demonstrate categorical winner switching across factors.
- Lack of synthetic winner switching should push the text toward performance-gap modulation, not claims that each structure selects a different best method.
- Edge-sharpness interactions should be described as weak or non-significant only if the generated `phase3_seed_interaction_tests.csv` rows confirm that after BH-FDR correction.
- Curvature must allow a non-monotonic interpretation; the analysis script intentionally does not force monotonicity.
- The original curvature-high amplitude 0.42 condition was invalid due to foreground collapse and must not be used as evidence.
- 3DGS-vs-GES-only comparisons remain pipeline validation because Gaussian is nested inside GES.
- Descriptor associations are not causal evidence.

## Potential Reviewer Weaknesses

- Real-scene comparisons are observational and confounded by implementation differences, training schedules, representation capacities, and primitive counts.
- DRK, GES, and 3DGS use different evaluation/rendering pathways; saved-model metrics must be used consistently.
- Patch-level analyses use overlapping patches; uncertainty cannot treat patches as independent samples.
- Five synthetic seeds are a useful pilot replication but still low-powered for subtle effects.
- Synthetic scenes may be too simple relative to natural scenes, especially if one method globally dominates all controlled conditions.
- LPIPS at small patch scale may be limited or inappropriate depending on the exact patch analysis context.
- Complexity descriptors are image-space proxies; they may not capture the true geometric or radiance-field factors driving method differences.
- Primitive-count differences complicate any direct representation-family comparison.
